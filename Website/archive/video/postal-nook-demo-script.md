# Postal Nook — Product Demo Video Script
**Runtime:** ~78 seconds · **6 scenes** · **Format:** Screen recording + VO inner monologue

---

## The Idea (read this first)

No talking head. No feature list. The screen is the star — real UI, real clicks, nothing faked.

Over it runs one woman's inner monologue. She's a skeptic. A friend ("H") told her to try this, and she's already decided it's going to be one of those products that *claims* to be simple. Her voice is dry, a little superior, mid-eye-roll.

The joke of the video is that **the product keeps finishing before her sentences do.** Every time she starts a complaint, the UI has already solved it. Her sarcasm doesn't get argued with — it just runs out of road.

**Her arc across six scenes:**

| Scene | Where her head is at |
|---|---|
| 1 | Smug. "This'll be painful." |
| 2 | Caught off guard. Still defending herself. |
| 3 | Actually curious. Sarcasm slipping. |
| 4 | Suspicious of the *catch* — and there isn't one. |
| 5 | Impressed against her will. |
| 6 | Converted. Slightly embarrassed about it. |

**Golden rule for the edit:** the cursor never hunts. It moves like someone who already knows where things are. Every click resolves in under a beat. If a screen takes longer to load than her line takes to say, cut the line — not the truth.

---

## SCENE 1 — The Login
**0:00 – 0:09 (9s)**

**On screen**
Clean login page, centered. Logo, email, password. Nothing else. Cursor arrives.

**Motion**
Email autofills. One click on *Sign in*. Dashboard resolves in a single soft fade — no spinner, no loading skeleton, no delay. The cut is almost too fast.

**Voiceover** *(dry, mid-eye-roll)*
> "Okay. I don't know why H keeps pushing this thing on me. Hot shot. I'm sure it's going to be *extremely easy* —"

*(She lands the air quotes. The dashboard is already on screen behind them. Beat.)*

> "…huh."

**Timing note**
The sign-in must complete **during** "extremely easy," not after. That overlap is the whole joke — protect it in the edit.

**Music**
Silence until 0:02. Then a single dry synth pluck, sparse, almost ironic. Low. No drums yet.

---

## SCENE 2 — The Dashboard
**0:09 – 0:22 (13s)**

**On screen**
Full dashboard. Overview cards up top. Three quick-links sitting right there: **Usage · Mail · Support**.

**Motion**
Slow, confident pan across the overview. Cursor hovers each quick-link in turn — each lifts slightly. No menus opened, no scroll hunting. Everything visible in one frame.

**Voiceover** *(recovering her attitude)*
> "Fine. Fast login. Big deal. Now comes the part where I go digging through eleven menus to find one thing —"

*(Cursor hovers Usage, then Mail, then Support. Three cards. Right there.)*

> "…or it's just three buttons. On the first screen."

**Timing note**
Hold the wide shot an extra beat after the last hover. Let the viewer see how little there is to learn.

**Music**
Same pluck picks up a second note. A soft kick enters on "three buttons." Momentum starts.

---

## SCENE 3 — Usage
**0:22 – 0:37 (15s)**

**On screen**
The Usage page. Six numbers, laid out plainly:

- Shredding Requests
- Mail Forwarding Requests
- Storage Days
- Scanned Pages
- Mail Items Received
- Number of Recipients

**Motion**
Each stat counts up in sequence, fast and staggered — roughly 0.15s apart. Cursor glides across them, doesn't click anything. Nothing needs clicking.

**Voiceover** *(curiosity leaking through)*
> "Alright, but where's the *real* data. The stuff you have to export to a spreadsheet and decode —"

*(The six counters finish. All of it is on one screen.)*

> "Shredded. Forwarded. Scanned. Stored. Received. Recipients. …That's everything I'd have asked for. That's just — everything."

**Timing note**
All six counters must land inside 4 seconds. Don't animate them one by one slowly; the point is that the full picture arrives at once.

**Music**
Full groove now. Warm, clean, forward-moving. The track has decided the argument is over even if she hasn't.

---

## SCENE 4 — Billing (and the exit that isn't hidden)
**0:37 – 0:52 (15s)**

**On screen**
Billing page. Current plan, exact charges, next billing date — all legible, no fine print. Cursor scrolls once. At the bottom: a clear, prominent **Unsubscribe** button. Not grey. Not buried. Not a link in 9pt type.

**Motion**
Scroll down. Cursor rests on Unsubscribe — hovers, doesn't click. Holds there for a full beat. Then moves away.

**Voiceover** *(this is the turn — suspicion, then genuine surprise)*
> "Here it is. The catch. This is where they hide the cancel button behind three support emails and a phone call —"

*(Cursor lands on the big, obvious Unsubscribe button. Long hover. She just… doesn't click it.)*

> "It's right there. They're not scared of me leaving."

**Timing note**
The hover is the emotional peak of the video. Give it a full second of stillness. Trust the silence.

**Music**
Drop everything except one sustained pad on "It's right there." Let the line breathe. Groove returns underneath the next scene.

---

## SCENE 5 — Business Services & Referral
**0:52 – 1:05 (13s)**

**On screen**
Business Services page. Three cards: **Get an EIN/ITIN · Form an LLC · Register an Agent.** Cursor opens one — clean form, few fields — then backs out. Quick cut to the **Refer a Friend** screen: priority support, evangelist perks.

**Motion**
Fast but never frantic. Card → form → back → referral. Cursor lands on the referral link and copies it. Small "Copied" toast appears.

**Voiceover** *(impressed, and slightly annoyed about it)*
> "Wait. It does the business stuff too? EIN. LLC. Registered agent. The three things I've been putting off since February."

*(Cut to referral. Link copied.)*

> "And apparently if I tell someone about it, I get priority support. …H. H, you absolute opportunist."

**Timing note**
This scene carries the most information — keep the VO tight and let the visuals do the listing. Don't narrate what's already legible on screen.

**Music**
Playful lift. Light percussion accent on the "Copied" toast.

---

## SCENE 6 — Support & Close
**1:05 – 1:18 (13s)**

**On screen**
Support page. Submit-a-request form — short, plain, no ticket taxonomy to learn. Submit. Confirmation. Cursor moves one tab over to **Ticket History**: past tickets, each with a visible status and update thread.

**Motion**
Type a short request (pre-typed, sped up). Submit. Green confirmation. One click to Ticket History — the previous tickets are already sitting there with statuses. Cursor stops. Slow pull back to the dashboard overview. Logo. End card.

**Voiceover** *(fully converted, dry again — but warm now)*
> "And when something does go wrong — a form. That's it. Then a history tab, so I'm not shouting into an inbox wondering if anyone read it."

*(Beat. Pull back to the dashboard.)*

> "…I'm going to have to text H. I hate this."

**End card**
**Postal Nook** — logo, then one line:
*Hundreds of people already stopped worrying about their mail.*
Below it: the CTA/URL. Hold 2 seconds. Cut to black.

**Music**
Resolves on the final line — one clean chord, then a soft tail under the end card. Doesn't fade awkwardly; it *lands*.

## Music Direction (one track, five gears)

One continuous piece that grows with her:

| Section | Feel |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:09 | Near-silence. A single dry pluck. Ironic, sparse. |
| 0:09–0:22 | Second note joins. Soft kick. Curiosity. |
| 0:22–0:37 | Full groove. Warm, clean, confident. |
| 0:37–0:44 | Strip to one pad. Space for the Unsubscribe beat. |
| 0:44–1:18 | Groove returns, lifts, resolves on the last line. |

**Search terms for licensing:** "minimal warm electronic," "playful tech underscore," "light indie synth, no vocals." Avoid corporate-uplifting stock — it fights the sarcasm and kills the joke.

---

## Production Notes

**Voice casting:** dry, unhurried, slightly amused. She's talking to herself, not to camera. The comedy comes from *underplaying* — no punching the sarcasm.

**Recording the screen:** capture at 60fps, real account with real (anonymized) data. Empty states break the illusion. Pre-fill Usage numbers, ticket history, and billing so nothing is at zero.

**Cursor:** slightly enlarged, subtle click highlights. Movements eased, never linear. It should feel like a confident hand, not a robot.

**Captions:** burn in the VO — most first views will be muted. Bottom-third, high contrast, one line at a time.

**Total: 1:18.** If you need to hit 60s, tighten Scene 5 (cut the LLC form open) and trim Scene 2's pan by 2s. Don't cut Scene 4 — that's the one that sells.
